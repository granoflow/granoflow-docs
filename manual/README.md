# Granoflow Manual

Granoflow helps you keep tasks, projects, reflection, and review cards in one workflow. You can collect work into tasks, place longer work inside projects and milestones, then keep useful experience as review material after the work is done.

This manual is written from the current app UI and reviewed screenshots. The first edition starts with review cards because they show one of Granoflow's core ideas clearly: a finished task does not have to disappear. Useful decisions, lessons, and questions can become material you can revisit later.

## Complete One Card Practice

**Doc type: Tutorial**  
**Reader goal:** Start from a fixed sample card and understand the question state, answer state, and rating buttons.  
**Completion criteria:** You can open a card, read the question first, show the answer, and choose a rating that matches how familiar it felt.

When you first open card practice, think of it as a small reuse loop. A question, decision, or lesson from a task, project, or reflection can become a prompt that is easier to recall later. The goal is not to memorize every sentence. The goal is to make a useful idea easier to find the next time you work.

This sample card asks, "What is a domain?" A domain is a long-running area of life or work that you care for, such as work, study, relationships, body, or rest. It reminds you that tasks are not just loose items. They usually belong to a broader direction.

![Question state in card practice: the card asks “What is a domain?” and the bottom button shows the answer action.](/manual/screenshots/en/review-card-study-domain-garden-question.png)

1. Open the card practice page and read only the question.
2. Try to answer in your own words before revealing the back.
3. Select **Show answer**.

After that, the page changes from question state to answer state. You will see the explanation on the back of the card and the rating buttons at the bottom.

![Answer state in card practice: the domain explanation is visible, with Forget, Hard, Good, and Easy rating buttons.](/manual/screenshots/en/review-card-study-domain-garden.png)

The rating does not need to look impressive. It records how retrieval felt this time:

- **Forget**: you could not recall it just now.
- **Hard**: you had some memory of it, but it was not stable.
- **Good**: you could recall the main idea.
- **Easy**: you could explain it without much effort.

If you are unsure, choose the rating closest to your real experience. Granoflow works better with honest feedback than with polished records. After several rounds, the cards help you see which ideas are becoming familiar and which ones should return to your tasks and projects for more work.

## Read Card Statistics

**Doc type: Reference**  
**Reader goal:** Know what the statistics page shows and how it helps you judge which cards still need practice.  
**Completion criteria:** You can read the total card count, today's practice count, and learning-state distribution.

The statistics page is useful for checking whether your card library is moving normally. It is not a scoreboard. It shows how many cards you have, how many you practiced today, and how many are still new, learning, mastered, or internalized.

![Card statistics page showing total cards, practiced today, and learning-state overview.](/manual/screenshots/en/review-card-statistics-main.png)

Read it this way:

- **Total cards**: how many cards are available for practice and review.
- **Practiced today**: how many practice actions were completed today.
- **Unseen / Learning / Mastered / Internalized**: where cards sit in the learning flow.

If the numbers do not match what you expect, first check that you are using the right account, device, and language. Then open card management to inspect individual cards.

## Manage Decks and Cards

**Doc type: How-to**  
**Reader goal:** Find the deck list, card management page, and note panel, and understand when to use each one.  
**Completion criteria:** You can distinguish decks, cards, and the note panel, and choose the right page for the task.

The deck list answers "Where did these cards come from, and how are they grouped?" It is also where deck import and export starts.

![Deck management page showing the deck list, import deck, and convert Anki deck actions.](/manual/screenshots/en/review-card-deck-list-main.png)

The card management page answers "What is happening with this specific card?" You can search cards and review active or archived content.

![Card management page showing search, active review, and archived sections.](/manual/screenshots/en/review-card-management-main.png)

When you are studying and want to return to the original context, open the note panel. It is useful when you want to see where a card came from before deciding what to do next.

![Note panel in card practice showing the note connected to the current card and related cards from the same note.](/manual/screenshots/en/review-card-study-note-panel.png)

A simple rule:

- To read overall quantity and state, open statistics.
- To organize by group, open the deck list.
- To find a specific card, open card management.
- To return to source context, open the note panel during practice.

## FAQ

**Why does the practice page show only one question first?**  
That is expected. The question state hides the answer so you can try retrieval once before checking the explanation.

**Does the rating affect future card rhythm?**  
The rating records how familiar the card felt this time. You do not need to choose a higher rating to make the record look good.

**What is the difference between a deck and a card?**  
A deck is an organizing group. A card is the specific item you practice. One deck can contain many cards.

**Why are some cards connected to tasks or notes?**  
Granoflow cards can come from tasks, projects, or notes. This keeps useful experience from one piece of work available for later review.

**Will this manual be expanded?**  
Yes. This first edition covers the card practice workflow. Later editions will add tasks, projects, reflection, data, sync, desktop features, and local interface chapters. Each expansion should be captured, reviewed, and then written into the manual.
