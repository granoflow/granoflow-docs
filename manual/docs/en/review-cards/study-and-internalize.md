---
title: Practice, Mastery, and Internalization
description: "Use card practice to recall past experience, and adjust review pacing based on how well you remember."
---

Card practice isn't about proving you have a good memory. Its purpose is simpler: before you actually do something, let those lessons you've already summarized surface again.

If you treat cards like a daily check-in you *have* to complete, they'll turn into a chore. A better approach: do a short round every day, and pick up the reminders you're most likely to forget or that could help you today.

## Try to recall first, then check the answer

![Card practice question](../../screenshots/en/review-card-study-domain-garden-question.png)

The question side only shows the prompt to give you a brief pause. When you see "What is a domain?", don't rush to tap the answer. Say it in your own words first: a domain is like a garden in your life, and tasks should be seen as part of a bigger life picture.

That pause matters. Just reading the answer gives you the feeling of "I get it", but you might not remember it next time you work on a task. Recall first, then correct — it sticks much better.

## Choose feedback based on how you actually felt

![Card practice back](../../screenshots/en/review-card-study-domain-garden.png)

After the answer is shown, four feedback buttons appear at the bottom. They aren't emotional ratings; they're signals that schedule the next practice round.

| Button | When to tap | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forgotten | Couldn't recall at all, or the answer was off track | This card needs to come back sooner |
| Tricky | Recalled a bit, but needed the answer's help | Not stable yet — keep practicing |
| Remembered | Mostly got it right, but delivery wasn't smooth | Continue at a normal pace |
| Easy | Recalled it effortlessly and know how to use it | Can wait longer before reappearing |

Don't tap "Easy" just to make your stats look good. If a card still doesn't help you in real tasks, honestly pick "Tricky" or "Remembered". GranoFlow isn't testing you — it's simply helping you schedule your next reminder.

## What's the difference between mastery and internalization

![Card statistics](../../screenshots/en/review-card-statistics-main.png)

"Mastered" and "internalized" on the statistics page aren't badges. Think of them this way:

| Status | What it's like | How you can use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not studied | A reminder you just put in your drawer | Pick the ones relevant today to practice |
| Learning | A reminder that still needs a few more encounters | Don't rush to judge success or failure |
| Mastered | You can consistently answer the question when you see it | Keep observing whether you use it in real tasks |
| Internalized | You often think of it naturally without looking at the card | You can shift your energy to newer experience |

A card's real value isn't in becoming "internalized"; it's in how it changes the way you handle your next task. For example, if you naturally start a task by asking, "Which domain does this belong to? Should I put it in a project?" — that means the card has moved from a reminder back into action.

## When to practice

You don't need to wait until you're in the perfect mood. The best time is usually short:

| Scenario | How to practice |
| --- | --- |
| Before starting work | Practice 3–5 cards to get into the flow |
| After completing a review | Practice cards related to the experience you just reviewed |
| Stuck on a project step | Search for relevant cards and revisit past judgments |
| Before the day ends | Do a short round to wrap up reminders that might be useful today |

If you find yourself repeatedly unable to answer a card during practice, it might not be your memory — the card itself might be too abstract. Go back to the card details and make the question more specific, or add an example to the answer.

## Don't turn practice into a stressor

Card learning should help you avoid stepping into the same pitfalls repeatedly, not add another item to your to‑do list. Practicing only two cards today is fine — what matters is whether those two cards helped you recall a concrete action.

A practical tip: after practice, pick the most useful card and ask yourself: **What's one thing today where I can use this?** If you can answer that, the practice has already landed.
